Section 4.1 — Temporary Resident Visas & eTA  |  Updated: 2026-10-01

Original Text

Updated Text

Electronic Travel Authorization (eTA)
  • The eTA is a digital entry requirement for visa-exempt foreign nationals (excluding U.S. citizens and U.S. Lawful Permanent Residents) who are traveling to or transiting through Canada by air.
  • While it is not required for entry by land (car/bus) or sea (cruise ship), an eTA is mandatory for boarding a flight to Canada and is electronically linked to a traveler's passport.
  • An eTA is typically valid for five years or until the linked passport expires, whichever occurs first.
  • Under the eTA Expansion program, citizens from select visa-required countries (such as the Philippines, Qatar, Indonesia, or Malaysia) may qualify for an eTA instead of a TRV if they have held a Canadian visa in the last 10 years or currently hold a valid U.S. non-immigrant visa.

Explanation

Qatar is a visa-exempt country, not a visa-required country.

Draft #p4 — Q111: Revised question (godson → cousin); correct answer D

Section 5.2 — Sponsorship — Family Class  |  MCQ Q111  |  Updated: 2026-10-01

Original Text

Updated Text

Sofia, a Canadian citizen, wants to sponsor her cousin under the "1 relative of any age" rule. She has no spouse, children, parents, grandparents, orphaned siblings, nieces, nephews, or grandchildren. Her only other relative is a living aunt who is a Canadian citizen. Can Sofia sponsor her cousin?

A.  Yes, because her cousin is considered like a child under sponsorship rules.

B.  No, because cousins are not a recognized family class relationship and cannot be sponsored under IRPR.

C.  Yes, because she has no spouse, children, or orphaned relatives.

D.  No, because the aunt's status as a Canadian citizen blocks the sponsorship.

Explanation

The question is revised so the correct answer is unambiguous: Sofia's living aunt (Canadian citizen) disqualifies her from the Lonely Canadian provision entirely — that is why D is correct. Under IRPR s.117(1)(h), the disqualifying list includes: spouse, child, parent, grandparent, sibling, uncle, aunt, great-uncle, great-aunt.

Note: The correct answer has been updated from B to D.
